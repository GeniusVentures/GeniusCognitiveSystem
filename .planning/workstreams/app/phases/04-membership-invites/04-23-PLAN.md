<objective>
Close the one remaining Phase 4 blocker: `RedeemInvite` and `Leave` read a raw member record, accept it on address/deleted checks only, and hand it to `WriteMemberPreservingAuthority`, which derives its Put key from the record body. Bind both raw reads to the requested scope so an honest node can never be made to write an unverified body into a different scope's member key, and prove it with a byte-level unit regression.

Purpose: the signed-authority chain (04-19..04-22) correctly excludes hostile records from every roster and authorization decision, but those two paths deliberately use the raw (unverified) reader to preserve authority bytes on cold nodes. That reader never checks that the body's `scope_id` matches the key it was read from, so a hostile membership-topic writer can publish `{scope_id: B, address: alice}` under `gcs/members/A/alice` and have Alice's leave/redeem in A copy those bytes into `gcs/members/B/alice` — overwriting B's valid record, or B's creator root, with bytes that cannot verify for B. That collapses B's verified authority until the creator re-signs and removes a member without approval (MEMB-02, MEMB-04). The fix is a scope binding at both propagation points plus the regression the verifier asked for.

Output: two scope rejections in `src/lib/gcs_membership.cpp`, a documented raw-read contract in `src/lib/gcs_membership.hpp`, and one new unit case in `test/test_gcs_membership.cpp` asserting both operations fail and `gcs/members/B/<sender>` is byte-identical afterwards.
</objective>

## Why the binding lives at the two call sites, not inside TryReadRawMember

The verifier preferred moving the check into `TryReadRawMember` so no future raw caller can repeat the defect. Every call site was enumerated; the helper-level option was rejected because it cannot satisfy both the required failure semantics and the existing write-once design without a contract change:

| Call site | Line | Today | If TryReadRawMember rejected a body/key mismatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| `VerifiedRoleFor` | 830 | Reads raw, then `VerifyMemberRecord` rejects at line 528 (`record.scope_id() != scopeId`) | Same failure — no observable change |
| `TryReadMemberVerified` | 847 | Same: `VerifyMemberRecord` binds at line 528 | Same failure — no observable change |
| `RedeemInvite` | 1589 | Merges the raw record and writes it under the body's key (defect) | `false` is indistinguishable from "no record": redeem would take the fresh-record branch, write a Guest record at A/key, and SUCCEED — the verifier's regression explicitly requires redeem to FAIL |
| `Leave` | 1931 | Mutates the raw record and writes it under the body's key (defect) | `false` makes leave fail — acceptable, but the same helper now silently changes the next row |
| `OnScopeCreated` (write-once root) | 2257 | Any occupying raw record, verifiable or not, blocks the root write (2252-2271) | A cross-scope body would read as "absent", so the creator root would OVERWRITE the occupying bytes — silently reversing the write-once decision and the accepted exclusion-not-rollback residual from 04-22 |

A tri-state helper (absent / rejected / ok) would fix Redeem's semantics and preserve write-once, but that is a private-helper contract change across five call sites and beyond this plan's minimal-fix boundary. The two call-site checks are therefore the minimal correct fix: they only reject a record the authority chain could never have produced at that key, and they leave every other raw caller untouched.

## Why the check cannot become a downgrade or a new DoS vector

- It adds no store read, no parse, no recursion, and no new error path: it compares two fields of the record already read and returns the same `Error::GcsDbError` every other rejection in these functions returns.
- A legitimate record cannot fail it: `WriteMemberAsAuthor` always stamps `record.scope_id()` from the scope it writes (921-979), and `VerifyMemberRecord` already requires the body/key binding for every read-based authority decision. A body naming another scope at this key can only come from a hostile or corrupt writer.
- `Leave`'s new rejection is strictly narrower than the current failure at `WriteMemberPreservingAuthority:986`: a legacy record with no `created_by`/`record_signature` already failed there, so the new check does not remove a capability that worked before.
- An attacker can still occupy A/alice with a hostile record and make Alice's leave/redeem fail; that availability impact already exists on the permissionless membership topic (per-key LWW, exclusion-not-rollback, 04-22 residual). The fix removes the far worse cross-scope write, and recovery remains the creator publishing the correct record.
